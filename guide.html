Gentle Steps: a short guide

Last updated: 4 November 2026

What it is for

You know what you need to do. Starting is the hard part.

Gentle Steps is an Android app for that moment. It was built with ADHD, autism and dyslexia in mind. It does not need a diagnosis, and it asks for none.

It is free, with a short advert before each set of steps. A paid plan removes the advert. It is Android only.

Get it on Google Play

What happens when you write a task

You write what you need to do, in your own words. For example: "make bread, go to the shops for the ingredients, do some studying".

An AI model turns it into small steps, in order.

Most steps get a time.

The AI can be wrong. Treat the steps as a place to start, not as instructions. You can add, change and delete tasks yourself, on any day, in the Plan tab.

The reminder and the gentle check

Each step with a time gets a real notification on your phone. A few minutes later, a quiet follow-up asks whether you have started. You choose how long it waits, or turn it off.

If now is not the moment, you can push the step back from the notification.

On a hard day, tap "Need a hand?". It keeps one step and moves the rest to tomorrow.

The list opens without a signal. Adding or changing a step needs a connection.

What it will not do

It has no streaks, no scores and no percentages. If you skip a day, the next morning offers yesterday's unfinished steps back once. You can take them on or let them go. Nothing is counted.

It does not connect to Outlook or Google Calendar.

It is a planning app, not a treatment or medical advice. It is not a crisis service. If things feel hard, your university or college wellbeing service or your doctor can help.

Reading settings

You can change the typeface and the background at any time. They are free, and always will be.

  • Atkinson Hyperlegible was drawn to tell apart letters that get mixed up, such as b, d, p and q. It is a design claim, not a treatment.
  • OpenDyslexic is offered for comfort. Careful studies have not found that it improves reading.
  • Warm is an amber tint for a lit room: softer than a cold white screen, which is the part of the eye-strain advice that holds up. It is not about blue light.
  • Blue is offered for comfort. There is little good independent evidence that it helps reading.
  • Night is a dark background, for low light.

Your data

  • What you type is sent to Anthropic, the company that makes the AI model, so the steps can be made. Anthropic does not use it to train its models. Your email address is not sent.
  • A short video advert from Google pays for the free steps. Google never receives what you type.
  • Nothing is sold.
  • Deleting: to delete your account, email us from the address you signed up with. It is done within 30 days. You can delete single tasks in the app yourself.

The privacy policy, the Terms and account deletion have the full detail. The evidence page rates the research behind each Learn tab strategy, weak evidence included.

For staff

To suggest it, send the Google Play link above. You could say: "A free Android app that turns a task into small steps, with reminders. It is not a treatment. It is one thing to try."

  • Gentle Steps sits beside the support your institution already gives. It does not replace it.
  • The app has no way to share a list.
  • Sign-up is an email address and a password. The app never asks about a condition.
  • The accessibility statement says what has been checked and what has not: it is self-assessed, with no independent audit yet and no testing by screen reader users.

Questions

Email gentlestepsapp@gmail.com. You get a reply from a person. For a group arrangement or a fuller assessment, say so. If something is hard to use, say what you were trying to do.
