Gentle Steps — Terms of Use

Last updated: 22 September 2026

These are the terms for using Gentle Steps. They are written to be read. If something here is unclear, ask rather than guess — gentlestepsapp@gmail.com.

The short version. Gentle Steps turns a goal you type into a few ordered steps with reminders. The steps come from an AI and can be wrong, so treat them as a starting point rather than instructions. It is a planning app, not health care or advice. You keep what you write. If you pay and something is not right, email and ask — refusing a refund to somebody who did not get what they paid for is not how this app is run.

Who this is for

Gentle Steps is for people aged 18 and over. That is what is declared on the Google Play listing and it is not a formality: the app has no parental controls and no separate handling for a child's data.

What you get for free

The free version gives you 35 breakdowns a month, all the reminders, the calendar, the Learn tab, and both reading settings — typeface and background. A short video advert plays before a free breakdown and is what pays for it.

If the advert fails to load, or none is available, you still get your steps. That is not your fault and you are not made to go without because of it. If you close the advert before it finishes, the breakdown is not generated. Nothing you typed is lost — it stays on screen and you can try again whenever you like. Gentle Steps Plus removes adverts altogether.

The reading settings stay free permanently. Charging disabled people for the typeface and background that make an app legible is not something this app will do.

Gentle Steps Plus

Plus is a subscription, billed through Google Play, at the price shown in the app before you confirm — that price includes any tax due where you live. It gives you:

  • No adverts. No video before a breakdown, ever.
  • 100 breakdowns a month rather than 35.
  • A week already decided — tidy-up tasks assigned to days, so there is nothing to work out.
  • Bin reminders that know which day yours are collected, including fortnightly rotations.
  • Help when you cannot start — four ways to shrink a task you are stuck on, applied to that task rather than offered as advice.
  • A day you can actually face — rework the whole list when it is too big, when there is only an hour, or when today is not that day.
  • A week, not just a day — something bigger spread across the days from whichever one you pick.
  • A home-screen widget showing your next step, so you do not have to open the app to know.

What is in Plus can change. If something is added you get it at no extra cost; if something is removed or moved out of Plus, the app will say so rather than quietly changing what you are paying for.

  • It renews automatically until you cancel it.
  • You can cancel any time in the Play Store: Play Store → your profile picture → Payments and subscriptions → Subscriptions.
  • Cancelling stops the next payment. You keep Plus until the date you have already paid for.
  • Where an introductory price applies, the app shows both that price and what it renews at before you confirm.

Deleting your account does not cancel your subscription. Google takes that payment, not us, so it keeps renewing until you stop it. Cancel first. This is said twice on purpose — on the account deletion page as well — because being charged for an app you deleted is exactly the kind of administrative injury this app exists not to inflict.

Refunds

Google Play handles payments, so refunds go through Google in the first instance. Play's own policy covers recent purchases directly in the Play Store.

Beyond that window, email and ask. You will get a refund where you were charged for something you did not mean to buy, where the app did not work as described, or where you subscribed and did not use it. You do not need to justify yourself at length.

If you subscribed and did not use it

You get back the time you have not used, not the time you have. The refund is worked out as a share of what you paid, by how much of the subscription is left.

So if you paid for a year and three months have gone by:

  • Nine of the twelve months are unused.
  • You get three quarters of what you paid, back.
  • The subscription ends there, and the app goes back to the free version — which keeps your tasks, your reminders and both reading settings.

Monthly subscriptions work the same way, though there is usually much less left to refund. If you simply want it to stop, cancelling in the Play Store before the next payment is the better move — that costs you nothing and needs no email.

This is more generous than required, deliberately. Forgotten subscriptions are a well-known financial harm for the people this app is built for, and an app about not punishing somebody's executive function should not profit from a lapse in it. None of this affects your statutory rights.

What the steps are, and what they are not

When you write a goal, the text is sent to an AI model which returns ordered steps and times. It can be wrong. It can misjudge how long something takes, put things in an order that does not suit you, or misunderstand what you meant entirely.

The steps are a suggestion to start from, not instructions to follow. You stay responsible for what you actually do — particularly anything involving your safety, your money, your health, or anyone else.

Not health care, and not advice

Gentle Steps is a planning app. It does not diagnose, treat, or manage any condition, and nothing in it is medical, psychological, legal or financial advice.

The Learn tab describes strategies with published evidence and says plainly how strong that evidence is, including where it is weak or absent. That is information, not a treatment plan. If you are struggling with your health, speak to a doctor.

What you write stays yours

Your goals and tasks are yours. They are stored so the app can show them back to you, and sent to the AI only so it can answer. They are not used to train any model, not sold, and not shared with advertisers. The privacy policy sets out exactly what is stored and who else sees it.

You can delete everything at any time — see the account deletion page.

Things not to do

  • Do not use the app to plan anything illegal, or anything intended to harm somebody.
  • Do not try to break, overload or work around the limits on the service, including the monthly allowance.
  • Do not resell access or share one account across many people. If you want to provide Gentle Steps to a group — a university disability service, for example — email and we will sort out a proper arrangement.

When things go wrong

This app is run by one person. It will sometimes be down, reminders will occasionally fail to fire, and the AI will occasionally produce nonsense. Please do not use it as the only thing standing between you and something that genuinely matters — a medication, a flight, a court date.

Nothing here limits liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, for fraud, or for anything else that cannot lawfully be excluded. Beyond that, and to the extent the law allows, liability is limited to what you have paid in the twelve months before the problem.

Your statutory rights as a consumer are not affected by anything on this page.

Ending it

You can stop using Gentle Steps whenever you like and delete your account from the page linked above.

Access may be withdrawn if the app is being used to break these terms — for the things in the list above, not for using it oddly or too much.

Changes

If these terms change in a way that affects what you get or what you pay, the app will say so rather than quietly updating this page.
